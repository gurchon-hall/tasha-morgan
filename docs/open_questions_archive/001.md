## OQ-1 - Prey/predator duel wording (status: resolved)

- Situation: with two players, each Methuselah is the other's prey
  and predator (Rulebook §5). Some card texts distinguish prey from
  predator, or refer to "other Methuselahs" in a way written for
  4-5 players.
- Rules/interactions: Rulebook §5's own worked example states
  verbatim, "each is prey of the other" — which by reciprocity (X is
  Y's predator iff Y is X's prey) also makes each the other's
  predator; the 2P variant's own exception list does not override
  this.
- Solution: confirmed as a rulebook fact, not an inference — with
  exactly two Methuselahs the sole opponent is simultaneously prey and
  predator. No card or engine path found so far needs to distinguish
  the two (every case collapses to "the opponent"); several modules
  (`engine/action.py`, `engine/ousting.py`, `cards/fleetness.py`,
  `cards/enchant_kindred.py`, `cards/line_brawl.py`,
  `cards/kine_resources_contested.py`) already cite this. A future
  card whose text assigns genuinely different effects to prey vs.
  predator specifically would need its own new OQ, not a reopening of
  this one.

## OQ-2 - Interface target (digital vs. physical) (status: resolved — digital first)

- Situation: whether the bot's interface targets a full digital engine
  or a physical-table workflow (the user transcribes every action and
  answers every impulse prompt).
- Rules/interactions: not a rules question — an architecture decision
  for the project.
- Solution: digital-first. The engine owns the full game state and
  exposes explicit legal choices; the first implementation target is a
  CLI interface. The physical-table workflow stays out of scope unless
  a concrete requirement appears.

## OQ-3 - Combat press step order (status: resolved)

- Situation: Rulebook §4 Combat names a "press" step each round but
  the condensed rules mapping didn't say who presses first.
- Rules/interactions: rulebook, fetched live, verbatim: "The acting
  minion always gets first opportunity to use cards or effects before
  the opposing minion" — stated as applying at every stage of combat,
  including press, matching the project's own impulse invariant.
- Solution: `engine/combat.py::run_combat`'s press step always orders
  `[(acting_player, acting_vampire), (blocking_player,
  blocking_vampire)]`, with no round-parity alternation.

## OQ-4 - Contested crypt card trigger moment (status: resolved)

- Situation: the 2P contested-crypt-card rule gave the resolution cost
  (1 pool/unlock or yield) but not the precise moment a contest
  begins.
- Rules/interactions: 2P variant page, verbatim: "If more than one
  unique crypt card with the same name is brought into play, that
  means control of the card is being contested... A second copy
  brought in later is immediately contested, and can still be used as
  normal."
- Solution: confirms the contest triggers on reveal into play, not
  while both copies are merely face down/uncontrolled.
  `src/vtesbot/engine/contests.py::detect_contest_on_reveal` already
  implemented this correctly — no code change needed.

## OQ-5 - Failed block attempt locking (status: resolved)

- Situation: whether a blocking vampire locks when a block attempt is
  merely made but fails to connect (stealth exceeds intercept), vs.
  only when the block actually succeeds.
- Rules/interactions: the rulebook's "Resolve the Action" step ties
  locking strictly to the action *being blocked* (a successful block):
  "the blocking minion is locked and enters combat with the acting
  minion." Combined with "If one attempt to block fails, another can
  be made as often as the blocking Methuselah wishes" (a locked minion
  could not retry), this confirms locking happens only on success.
- Solution: `src/vtesbot/engine/action.py::attempt_block` already
  implements this correctly — no code change needed.

## OQ-6 - Referendum vote tally for two Methuselahs (status: resolved)

- Situation: the condensed rules mapping gave vote sources and "terms
  chosen only after success" but not what votes are compared against,
  what "ties fail" means, or whether "automatically passing"
  ([PIB 20150105]/[LSJ 19980107], printed on Perfect Paragon/Scalpel
  Tongue) is a duel-specific mechanic.
- Rules/interactions: rulebook, fetched live: casting happens "in any
  order," tallied for/against, ties fail — this degrades cleanly to
  2P (an uncontested referendum is 0-for/0-against, which ties, same
  as any player count); the 2P variant has no referendum-procedure
  override beyond the already-known title-contest exception;
  "automatically passing" (General Ruling [PIB 20150105]) is a
  card-text-triggered category (a card whose own text declares its
  called referendum passes automatically), unrelated to player count —
  none of this pool's three Political Action cards carry that text.
- Solution: `political_action` calls `resolve_referendum`
  (`engine/politics.py`), which runs a pre-cast `"polling_step"` hook
  window, collects every vote source (the caller's own card vote —
  offered as a real decision, not hardcoded — titled ready vampires
  via `TITLE_VOTES`, an Edge-burn source, any hook-added source, and a
  non-calling Methuselah's own 1-vote burn-from-hand of a different
  political action card), lets each cast for/against/abstain in
  table-order rotation (OQ-20), dispatches a post-tally
  `REFERENDUM_TALLIED_HOOK`, and tallies (ties/0-0 fail).
  `REFERENDUM_RESOLVED` is reserved for a later, fully-post-resolution
  trigger (Voter Captivation), not yet dispatched. Hook contexts carry
  only `caller_vampire_id`, never the live object (information
  hiding). Disputed Territory, Kine Resources Contested, Parity Shift,
  Alexa Draper, Diana Iadanza's "+1 bleed" clause, and Marcos Belegrad
  now have everything the engine needs but remain `blocked` pending a
  `card-implementer` pass; Perfect Paragon/Queen Anne need further
  card-specific work; Voter Captivation needs `REFERENDUM_RESOLVED`'s
  own dispatch call site built first. User ruling: declining the
  caller's own clause-1 vote does not unlock the clause-2
  burn-from-hand option for the caller — the calling card consumes
  that vote slot the instant it calls the referendum, regardless of
  whether the automatic vote is later cast or declined.

## OQ-7 - 47th Street Royals - no ally-in-play entity (status: resolved)

- Situation: 47th Street Royals (102217, Ally, Brujah, "You can burn
  47th Street Royals to reduce a bleed against you by 3") was blocked
  — the engine had no representation of an ally in play at all.
- Rules/interactions: plain card text, not a ruling ambiguity; needs a
  minion-like entity with its own life/strength/bleed, distinct from a
  vampire, that can be brought into play, act independently, and be
  destroyed or burned by its own card text.
- Solution: added `AllyInPlay` (`engine/state.py`) and
  `engine/allies.py::recruit_ally`/`burn_ally`; the card's own
  reaction was proven against the existing `"bleed_amount_modifier"`
  hook with `burn_ally` as the cost. Also added `AllyView`/
  `Observation.allies` (an ally in the ready region is fully public,
  same as a ready vampire — Rulebook §4 Recruit Ally has no face-down
  state for an ally). Not built: the full "recruit ally" minion action
  itself (tracked as OQ-12) and ally bleed/block/combat participation.
  Card stays `blocked`.

## OQ-8 - Celerity - attachment effects unconsulted (status: resolved)

- Situation: Celerity (100312, Discipline master card): "+1 level of
  Celerity [cel], +1 capacity. Cannot be put on a vampire with
  superior Celerity [CEL]." Attaching the card was already wired
  (`CardAttachment`, the `"master_phase_play"` hook); its two numeric
  effects had no consumer — every capacity/discipline-level read in
  the engine read `vampire.card.*` directly, bypassing
  `VampireInPlay.attachments`.
- Rules/interactions: plain printed text, no ruling ambiguity — the
  generic "Discipline"-type master-card pattern (one per discipline).
- Solution: added `engine/attachments.py::effective_capacity`/
  `effective_disciplines`, two new passive hooks
  (`"capacity_modifier"`, `"discipline_level_modifier"`); updated
  every `engine/` reader (`damage.py::add_blood`, both
  `phases/influence.py` capacity checks,
  `observation.py::VampireView.capacity`) to consult them instead.
  Celerity's own card module stays `blocked`; `threats.py`/
  `telepathic_counter.py` still read the raw field directly, flagged
  for a future pass.

## OQ-9 - Bonding - stealth_modifier omits action type (status: resolved)

- Situation: Bonding (100236)'s superior clause grants "+1 stealth,"
  usable only during a bleed action — but the `"stealth_modifier"`
  hook context carried no field saying which action (bleed vs. hunt)
  was in progress, so a provider could not correctly gate on it.
- Rules/interactions: card text and rulings ([TOM 19951109],
  [LSJ 19980824]/[RTR 19941109]) are explicit and consistent — not a
  ruling ambiguity, a missing engine capability.
- Solution: threaded an `action: str | None` discriminator through
  `perform_minion_action`/`attempt_block`/`_duel_stealth_intercept`
  into the stealth/intercept hook context and the block-attempt
  decision; production call sites pass named constants
  `ACTION_BLEED`/`ACTION_HUNT`/`ACTION_POLITICAL` (added so a future
  typo can't silently fail to match a provider's own check). Card
  itself stays `blocked` (see OQ-11 for the remaining gap).

## OQ-10 - Contested unique allies lack hidden state (status: resolved)

- Situation: `AllyView`/`build_observation` showed every in-play ally
  unconditionally to both players — correct for a non-unique ally but
  not for a contested unique one.
- Rules/interactions: Rulebook §4 Advanced Rules > Contested Cards is
  one unified mechanism for every unique card, crypt or not (face
  down, out of play, 1 pool/unlock-phase or yield); the 2P variant's
  "stays in play, usable" override is scoped to crypt cards only (by
  its own title and aside) — so a contested ally follows the
  unmodified rulebook rule. A recruited ally is itself played face up,
  so unlike an uncontrolled vampire a contested ally's identity is
  never actually secret — no anonymization branch is needed, only a
  zone/usability marker.
- Solution: added `AllyInPlay.contested_with` and a `"contested"` zone
  value; `engine/allies.py::detect_contest_on_recruit` (mirrors the
  vampire-side function), wired into `_perform_recruit_ally`;
  `AllyView.contested: bool`. A pool sweep found six more unique
  allies plus one unique Equipment (Shilmulo Tarot) sharing this gap.
  The resolution loop itself (pay/yield) was deliberately left to
  OQ-15; Equipment-side tracking is left open (no card needs it yet).

## OQ-11 - Bonding - carrying an early hook effect forward (status: resolved)

- Situation: Bonding's superior clause must produce two effects from
  one play — "+1 stealth" (usable only in the earlier block-attempt
  window) and "+1 bleed" (only readable by the later bleed-amount
  window) — and nothing in the engine let one hook application leave
  something for a later one to pick up within the same action.
- Rules/interactions: not a ruling ambiguity (OQ-9 already settled the
  action-type gating); a missing engine primitive. Applying the bleed
  bonus immediately instead (bypassing the later clamp) was rejected:
  it desynchronizes from the amount-window's floor-at-0 clamp and
  Edge-holder check, overcharging the defender in a worked example.
- Solution: `perform_minion_action` now builds a shared, mutable
  `pending: dict` before `attempt_block` runs, threaded into both the
  stealth/intercept hook context and the later bleed-amount-modifier
  context; `_perform_bleed` folds `pending[PENDING_BLEED_AMOUNT_KEY]`
  into the base amount before the clamp. Bonding was then implemented
  against both providers; a related cross-card "(limited)" bookkeeping
  gap (Threats vs. Bonding's superior clause) was fixed by re-keying
  `bleed_bookkeeping` off `pending` instead of the later-window-only
  `amount_box`. `Status.IMPLEMENTED`.

## OQ-12 - 47th Street Royals - recruit-ally action not wired (status: resolved)

- Situation: OQ-7 built the ally representation, but no minion action
  let a player actually recruit one in a real game —
  `_choose_and_perform_action` only ever offered bleed/hunt.
- Rules/interactions: Rulebook §4 "Recruit Ally": any ready minion,
  cost as listed on the card, undirected, +1 stealth; a played card is
  set aside (not yet ash-heaped) until the action resolves, burned
  only on a block (cost not paid).
- Solution: added a "recruit ally" minion action generalizing
  `perform_minion_action` with an `on_blocked` callback (fires instead
  of `resolve` on a block, after combat), `RecruitAllySpec` as the
  hook-option contract, and `play_from_hand_pending` (card leaves hand
  immediately but isn't ash-heaped until the action resolves). Folded
  into the same top-level action-choice decision as bleed/hunt. 47th
  Street Royals' own card module stays `blocked` (its own provider is
  a follow-up); deliberately not extended to allies-as-actor (no card
  needs it, and allies aren't unlocked by the engine yet).

## OQ-13 - 47th Street Royals - recruit_ally_play omits acting vampire (status: resolved)

- Situation: a card's clan requirement (47th Street Royals requires
  Brujah) binds to the specific minion playing it, not merely "the
  Methuselah controls some vampire of that clan" — but the
  `"recruit_ally_play"` hook's context carried no identifier for which
  of possibly several ready-unlocked vampires was acting.
- Rules/interactions: Rulebook §2 "Requirements for Playing Cards"
  draws the minion-card vs. master-card distinction explicitly; a
  minion card (which Ally cards are) binds to the acting minion
  itself.
- Solution: `_choose_and_perform_action` now also passes
  `vampire=vampire.instance_id` into the hook's offer context, letting
  a provider look up the specific acting vampire and check its clan.
  Card module itself stays `blocked` for a future pass.

## OQ-14 - 47th Street Royals - recruit makes contested-ally gap live (status: resolved)

- Situation: with OQ-12/OQ-13 resolved, two independently-built
  2P-legal decks could both recruit their own copy of 47th Street
  Royals — making OQ-10's then-still-open uniqueness gap reachable in
  an ordinary game, not merely latent.
- Rules/interactions: same Contested Cards rule as OQ-10; shipping
  as-is would have silently created two independent, fully-visible
  `AllyInPlay` instances under two controllers instead of the
  mandated face-down/out-of-play treatment.
- Solution: resolved by OQ-10's own fix landing (contest detection on
  recruit). The remaining piece — actually *resolving* a contest once
  created (pay/yield) — was deliberately left to OQ-15. Card stays
  `blocked`.
